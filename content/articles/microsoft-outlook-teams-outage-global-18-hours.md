A Microsoft outage that began on Thursday, November 12, disrupted Outlook email and Teams video conferencing and spread to other cloud services worldwide, with traffic returning only on Friday after roughly 18 hours of downtime, TechCrunch reported.

## What was affected

The problem was first flagged in the United States and the Americas and then widened as engineers worked to bring downed servers back. Affected services included:

- Consumer and business email
- Cloud files and documents
- Copilot AI tools
- Defender security screening
- Intune administration dashboards
- Access to some cloud-based Windows computers

Internet analyst Doug Madory of Kentik told TechCrunch that traffic to Microsoft services was coming back after about 18 hours.

## Cause still unexplained

Microsoft has not said what triggered the outage. It described the issue as "a large number of servers being unexpectedly unavailable" and said it was still investigating. TechCrunch reported that engineers initially could not identify the cause. A Friday status update said there was "no evidence of malicious activity associated with this incident." A Microsoft spokesperson did not say whether the company can verify that through logs or other detection, according to the report.

## Context

This follows a multi-day Microsoft outage in September 2026, which the company attributed to a "core authentication configuration" problem. TechCrunch noted Microsoft is likely to face questions over how long recovery took.

Microsoft's cloud also underpins other businesses' email and collaboration, so incidents of this kind can reach many industries at once. Microsoft has not yet published a post-incident analysis.
