Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter have agreed to closed-door, transcribed interviews with three House committees on December 1 and December 2, respectively, CNBC reported on Monday, as U.S. officials widened scrutiny of the company's China chip sales.

## The congressional interviews

Huang resigned as chief executive on November 12. The interviews are being held by House committees on Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and China, according to Axios and other outlets. The sessions will not be televised, and NBC News reported that transcripts could be released later. Ryan Fedasiuk, a former State Department adviser, told CNBC there is "a very good chance" investigators will find that Nvidia personnel knew its products could be used for restricted purposes.

## The Megaspeed investigation

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told ABC on Sunday that "there is an investigation ongoing into her firm and into her," referring to Alice Huang and Megaspeed, the Singapore-based cloud firm she formerly worked for. CNBC reported that:

- Commerce and Justice officials said last week that the Megaspeed probe began in 2024 over potential export-control violations.
- The Justice Department later took the lead on the criminal investigation.
- The New York Times reported that Alice Huang was in contact with an officer of a top Chinese intelligence agency from spring 2025 through fall 2026, citing people briefed on U.S. intelligence reports. CNBC did not independently confirm that.
- Singapore's Home Affairs ministry said earlier this month that police found insufficient evidence of offenses tied to possible inaccurate declarations, and that she received a stern warning. It did not investigate her for espionage.

Megaspeed says it is a "legitimate and lawful business" and that Alice Huang left in August 2025. CNBC described her as having reportedly had a romantic relationship with Jensen Huang.

## Treasury seeks answers

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CBS on Sunday that the administration wants answers from Nvidia about its China chip sales, including who oversaw compliance. Separately, The Wall Street Journal reported that Li Ming, a Chinese national out on bail in Singapore, was "deeply involved" in Megaspeed's decisions; he faces fraud-related charges in Singapore that do not name Megaspeed.

The allegations come from officials and anonymous sources, and no charges against Nvidia or the Huangs have been announced.
