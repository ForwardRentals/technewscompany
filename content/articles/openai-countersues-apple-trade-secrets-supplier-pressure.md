OpenAI has filed a countersuit against Apple in a California court, alleging that Apple stole its trade secrets and tried to cut it off from hardware suppliers, according to reports on November 27 citing the Financial Times. The claims are OpenAI's allegations and have not been proven in court.

## What OpenAI alleges

OpenAI says Apple is "coercing and threatening" suppliers that work with it, as OpenAI develops consumer devices following its 2025 acquisition of Jony Ive's io company. According to the report, the filing says Apple is trying to "compete unfairly by any means available."

- Apple encouraged employees to store corporate files on personal devices and cloud accounts, OpenAI alleges.
- In October, OpenAI said a former employee who had returned to Apple sent more than 100 confidential files about unreleased OpenAI hardware to personal accounts. The files reportedly included device images, engineering data and supplier information.
- OpenAI has previously said Apple's original case is an attempt to stop it from hiring Apple employees.

## Apple's response

After the report, Apple said its case concerns OpenAI employees "deliberately stealing, accessing, and using" its confidential information, and described the counterclaims as "a transparent attempt to distract from the substance of Apple's lawsuit." Apple also said OpenAI has already been ordered to stop using Apple's confidential information. In October, Apple said its own investigation of OpenAI's earlier claim had found no OpenAI confidential information.

## The underlying case

Apple sued OpenAI, io Products and former Apple employees Chang Liu and Tang Tan in July 2026. Tan previously led iPhone and Apple Watch product design. Apple alleges confidential engineering and hardware information was taken to benefit OpenAI's consumer device work, which OpenAI disputes.

- Apple has sought a preliminary injunction to stop further use of the alleged trade secrets.
- Apple obtained expanded access to evidence during discovery in October 2026.
- Trial in Apple's original case is scheduled for July 19, 2027.
