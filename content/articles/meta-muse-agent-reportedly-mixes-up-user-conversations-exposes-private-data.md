Meta employees have raised concerns internally that the company's Muse AI agent mixes up conversations between accounts and displays private information to the wrong person, TechCrunch reported on November 19, citing a Reuters investigation that reviewed internal Meta posts. The claims come from internal company posts and have not been independently confirmed.

## What employees described

According to the report, staff said Muse surfaced content they had never requested, including:

- Childcare requests
- Career advice
- Product orders
- Bank account balances belonging to someone else

In one example quoted in the report, Muse added an unrelated message to a conversation about weather and tides, listing two bank balances. Another employee, using Muse on a personal phone, received a reminder to reply to her sister's message on Signal. She called it "an extreme privacy violation" and said she had not given the agent access to other apps.

## Meta's response

A Meta spokesperson said employee testing is central to product development and supplies feedback used to improve safety and privacy protections. The spokesperson added that employees have access to different versions of Muse than the public, so they may see features and behavior that are not available to users. The statement does not say whether the described incidents involved real customer data or test accounts.

## Context

Muse launched in September and, according to TechCrunch, is one of the fastest-growing apps on record, with more than 10 million downloads. Meta has been expanding the Muse family, including an open-weight release covered earlier on this site.

The report matters because agents that act across a user's messages, accounts and apps depend on strict separation between users' data. Pre-release testing exists to catch problems like those described, but the account suggests the boundaries between test and public builds are an open question. Meta has not published details on how many conversations were affected.
