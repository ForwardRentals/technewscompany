Data center developer Crusoe confirmed on Friday that it has sold an unfinished data center project in southeastern Wyoming to Google, TechCrunch reported. Google did not disclose the purchase price.

## The deal

Crusoe announced the project in 2025 with Blackstone-backed Tallgrass, with plans to scale it to 10 gigawatts. Google had tried to become a customer of the project but did not sign on, and it is now taking over development. TechCrunch reported that Google had raised concerns about cost and construction timeline under Crusoe's management. Bloomberg first reported the sale in June.

Google has announced plans to invest $17 billion to develop the campus.

## Crusoe's wider business

Crusoe, a Denver-based developer founded eight years ago, remains a Google partner and is building another data center for the company near Amarillo, Texas. TechCrunch listed other key projects and customers:

- A campus in Abilene, Texas, built in phases for Oracle and OpenAI
- An adjacent 900-megawatt Abilene campus for Microsoft, with the first building expected online in mid-2027
- Meta as a customer
- A reported $13 billion, five-year cloud deal with Jane Street, according to Bloomberg

The company was valued at $30.9 billion and raised a $3.9 billion round in September, after the Wyoming setback.

## Why it matters

The sale illustrates how difficult it can be for independent developers to deliver very large AI campuses on time and on budget, given constraints on equipment and power, and sometimes local opposition, TechCrunch wrote. It also shows a hyperscaler stepping in to control a project directly rather than leasing capacity from a third-party builder.
