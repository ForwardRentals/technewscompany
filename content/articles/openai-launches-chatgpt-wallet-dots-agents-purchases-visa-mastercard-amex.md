OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, Nov. 13, a payment feature that lets the company's "dots" AI agents buy items on a user's behalf, CNBC reported. The first release is limited to U.S. subscribers of OpenAI's Pro plan who are over 18.

## How it works

Users enter card details once, ask a dot to find items to buy, and approve each purchase through a security check. According to CNBC, the agents never see full card details. Instead, the payment networks supply a transaction-specific token containing a scoped card number, security code and expiration date.

- OpenAI built the product with American Express, Mastercard and Visa.
- Cardholders keep their existing card rewards and protections.
- OpenAI says it is not taking a share of transactions for now, though purchases could become a revenue source later.
- The company published tools and guidance for merchants, including how to avoid inadvertently blocking its agents.

Chaya Nayak, who leads monetization and payments product at OpenAI, said the company wants to "start building out a really great foundation that's safe and easy to use." She added that the audience for now is "the networks and the merchants." Amex executive Luke Gebb said a limited rollout makes sense: "start it in a contained environment, and then keep rolling from there."

## Context

OpenAI introduced the dots agents at its developer conference in late September. CNBC reports ChatGPT had more than 1.2 billion weekly active users and nearly 100 million paying users at the end of the third quarter. The company's earlier Instant Checkout feature, launched last fall, was moved away from, and a revamped shopping experience followed in March.

## Merchant resistance

Not every retailer is open to agent purchases. CNBC notes that United Airlines has said it will block agents from its systems for booking tickets, and Amazon prevents agents from buying products on its platform. OpenAI describes agentic commerce as being in its "early days."

## What to watch

The company has not said when Wallet will reach other plans or countries. Merchant acceptance, and who is responsible when an agent makes a mistake, remain open questions for the category.
