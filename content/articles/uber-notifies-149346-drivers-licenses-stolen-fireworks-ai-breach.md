Uber is notifying nearly 150,000 drivers that personal data, including driver's license details, was stolen in a breach at a third-party vendor, TechCrunch reported on November 16. A filing with the Oregon attorney general says 149,346 people were notified.

## What was taken

The exposed data includes names, addresses, dates of birth and driver's license numbers. Uber said the vendor, Fireworks AI, which gives developers access to open-weight models, told it of the incident on October 9. Uber described the event as "a security incident impacting one of our third-party vendors, Fireworks.ai."

## How it happened

Hackers broke into Fireworks, stole internal credentials and used them to reach Uber's data. Uber said it had alerts set up for unauthorized access to its Fireworks environment, but because the intruders used Fireworks' own credentials, "no alerts were triggered."

- Uber filed notices with several U.S. attorneys general, including California and Iowa
- TechCrunch has also seen notifications in Illinois, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Texas and Vermont
- Uber deferred questions to Fireworks and did not say whether the number of affected drivers could grow

## The wider Fireworks incident

Fireworks reported its own breach in October, in which attackers took customer billing data and some private AI datasets. Notion and Cursor have confirmed their data was taken. A Fireworks spokesperson confirmed unauthorized access to some systems that affected customer data but did not answer detailed questions. Fireworks blamed a financially motivated criminal group that seeks to sell stolen tokens for accessing large language models; no known group has claimed responsibility.

## Context

TechCrunch noted this is Uber's second known breach this year. In August, an extortion gang claimed to have stolen data from Uber Freight, the company's trucking unit.
