Palo Alto Networks is reportedly in talks to acquire Clutch Security, a startup that secures AI agents' access to company systems, for $500 million in cash plus milestone-based payments, according to TechCrunch, which cited a person familiar with the discussions. The Information first reported the talks. Neither company responded to TechCrunch's requests for comment, and no deal has been announced.

## About Clutch

TechCrunch described Clutch as a three-year-old startup focused on controlling what AI agents can reach inside corporate environments. Key figures from the report:

- Clutch was last valued at $85 million when it raised a $20 million Series A in January 2025, led by Lightspeed and SignalFire.
- It is approaching $10 million in annual recurring revenue.
- At $500 million, the price would be roughly 50 times that revenue.

## Palo Alto's acquisition pace

If completed, the deal would be Palo Alto Networks' seventh acquisition this year, according to PitchBook data cited by TechCrunch. In September the company bought Console, an AI-agent IT help-desk startup, for $500 million in stock and cash. Other 2026 purchases listed from regulatory filings include:

- Embrace, for $325 million
- Chronosphere, for $2.95 billion
- Koi, for $231 million

## Market context

Securing AI agents has become a crowded acquisition target. TechCrunch noted that Cyera, valued at $12 billion earlier this year, bought Oasis Security for $1 billion, and that Cisco bought Astrix for roughly $400 million, according to Calcalist. Upwind reportedly turned down a Datadog offer of around 40 times annual recurring revenue.

Because the talks are unconfirmed, the price and structure could change or the deal could fall through.
