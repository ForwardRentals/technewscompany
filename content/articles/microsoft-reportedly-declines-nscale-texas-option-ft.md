Microsoft has reportedly chosen not to exercise an option that would have more than doubled its compute contract with UK neocloud Nscale at a Texas site, according to the Financial Times, as relayed by Silicon UK on November 9, 2026. The article describes the deal as one that would have been worth tens of billions of dollars.

## The details

Citing unnamed sources, the FT said the option covered about 500MW of capacity at Nscale's own data centre in Ward County, Texas.

- In October 2025, Nscale said it would lease Microsoft about 240MW at an adjacent site being developed by crypto miner Ionic Digital, with an option to expand.
- Nscale's own site has 500MW of power capacity; the Ionic site is expected to add 700MW, with Nscale holding first refusal on finding customers.
- Power for the Ionic site has not yet been secured. Microsoft still holds a right of first refusal there, according to Ionic's SEC filings.
- Robotics company Figure said in September 2025 it would lease capacity at the Ward County site, and the FT reports other firms have signed deals there.

## Context

The report comes as neocloud providers such as Nscale, Nebius and CoreWeave face investor scepticism over debt-funded expansion and higher borrowing costs. Last month Microsoft reportedly also pulled out of participating in a major Nscale data centre in Essex, England, after power delivery to that site was delayed.

Silicon UK separately reported that Nscale is considering delaying its planned IPO. Neither Microsoft nor Nscale is quoted in the coverage reviewed, and the details above rest on the FT's anonymous sourcing.
