Tesla said in a post on X that its robotaxi service became available in San Antonio at 5 a.m. local time on Monday, making it the company's fourth Texas city, according to TechCrunch.

## What Tesla has said

Tesla did not give operating hours, did not say whether the service uses its two-seat Cybercab or modified Model Y vehicles, and did not say whether human safety monitors are on board. Based on earlier launches, TechCrunch expects a limited fleet in a confined area that expands gradually.

## Where Tesla operates

- Austin: launched June 2025 with safety operators; monitors removed from Austin robotaxis in January 2026
- Limited areas of Miami and Orlando in Florida
- Dallas, Fort Worth and Houston in Texas
- San Francisco Bay Area: a ride-hailing service with drivers behind the wheel, run under a transportation charter permit; Tesla lacks the permits for driverless ride-hailing in California

Texas registration data and the Robotaxi Tracker site show more than 400 autonomous vehicles registered in Austin, while other cities have only a handful to a few dozen. Fort Worth has 24 vehicles and Miami five, TechCrunch reported.

## Competition and context

Waymo operates driverless robotaxis in Austin, Dallas, Houston and San Antonio, and recently resumed San Antonio service after a five-month pause that followed incidents in which its vehicles became stuck on flooded roads. TechCrunch also noted a July report that Tesla's network logged fewer paid-customer miles in the second quarter than in the first.
