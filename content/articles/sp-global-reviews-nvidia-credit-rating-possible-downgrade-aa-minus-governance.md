S&P Global Ratings said on November 25 that it is considering lowering Nvidia's credit rating from AA to AA-, citing management and governance concerns after the removal and reinstatement of chief executive Jensen Huang, TechCrunch reported.

## S&P's concerns

According to the report, S&P pointed to "weak executive oversight and lack of succession planning" and to "key man risk," meaning the company's heavy dependence on Huang. It also noted Nvidia's strong financial performance. S&P expects to finish its review within 90 days, and Nvidia would be expected to remain investment grade even if the rating is cut. TechCrunch's article did not include a statement from Nvidia.

## Why a rating matters here

A lower rating could raise Nvidia's borrowing costs. It could also matter beyond Nvidia's own balance sheet, because the company backstops so-called neoclouds and other AI infrastructure firms, whose debt could become more expensive or require more collateral.

Key background:

- In August, Nvidia announced plans to arrange $500 billion of AI infrastructure financing with six institutions, including Goldman Sachs, Apollo and Blackstone.
- Under the original terms, Nvidia would guarantee 25% of the loans.
- Bloomberg reported that lenders were pressing for larger guarantees even before Huang's departure and return, and TechCrunch said a downgrade would likely strengthen those demands.

## Context

Nvidia's board reinstated Huang as CEO on November 23, and shares rallied that day before closing lower later in the week. The S&P review adds a credit angle to a week of questions about the company's governance and the financing of the AI build-out.
