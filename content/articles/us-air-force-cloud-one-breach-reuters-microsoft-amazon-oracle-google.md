Hackers have breached Cloud One, one of the U.S. Air Force's main cloud computing platforms, according to a Reuters report on November 27 that cites five anonymous sources. Reuters could not identify the attackers, determine how much of the platform was affected, or confirm whether any data was stolen.

## What was reported

Cloud One began in 2017 as a one-stop shop for Air Force IT needs and combines resources from four cloud operators: Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet's Google. It runs applications for personnel data, pay, logistics and maintenance.

- Staff reportedly discovered the intrusion about a month ago while working on an upgrade of the platform.
- One source said attackers exploited a software flaw to reach the control plane, which manages the platform's main functions; the source compared it to a universal remote control.
- Investigators reportedly asked U.S. Cyber Command to assess the damage. Cyber Command did not respond to Reuters.

## Response and impact

According to the sources, efforts to migrate back-office applications to Cloud One have been paused, and some users had administrator rights revoked. Updates to applications already on the platform were blocked, so personnel could not install fixes as a new batch of serious software vulnerabilities was disclosed across the industry.

In a statement, the Air Force said it does not comment on the security posture, vulnerabilities or operational status of specific networks, and that it continuously takes measures to protect its information, systems and mission capabilities. SAIC, the main contractor until 2025, did not respond to Reuters, and Leidos, which took over that year, referred questions to the Air Force.

## Context

Reuters named two applications on the platform: Cloud-Based Command and Control, which combines air defense radar data across North America, and the Theater Integrated Combat Munitions System, which tracks munitions at more than 200 Air Force locations worldwide. Reuters linked the incident to growing concern about AI-assisted intrusions, and said the U.S. government is already handling at least six other recently disclosed incidents. None of the cloud providers' roles in the breach was described in the reporting.
