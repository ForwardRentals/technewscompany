The UK's Investigatory Powers Tribunal on Thursday ordered the British government to confirm that it issued a secret order requiring Apple to give authorities access to encrypted customer data, TechCrunch reported. The independent court, which hears challenges to government surveillance, rejected the Home Office's argument that acknowledging the order would harm national security.

## What the tribunal decided

The case was brought by the civil liberties group Liberty on behalf of two individuals. Liberty said it is the first known time the government has been compelled to confirm the existence of a secret technical order, according to the report.

The Home Office has not itself confirmed that the notice exists and did not respond to TechCrunch's request for comment. Apple, which had been legally barred from discussing the order even after details leaked, said it welcomes the public confirmation.

## Background

Roughly a year ago, reports said the UK had issued a "technical capabilities notice" demanding that Apple build a way for UK authorities to reach customer data worldwide. US officials and lawmakers criticized the demand, warning it could put Americans' privacy at risk. The order was later reported to have been narrowed to cover the data of British users in Apple's encrypted cloud storage, and Apple challenged that version as well.

- Apple declined to comply and instead stopped UK users from turning on Advanced Data Protection, its end-to-end encryption option for iCloud.
- Apple has said it has "never built a backdoor or master key" to any of its products or services.
- A final hearing on which details of the order can be made public is expected in 2027.

## Why it matters

Technical capability notices are issued under strict non-disclosure rules, which has meant companies and the public have had little formal confirmation of when they are used. The ruling does not decide whether the order itself is lawful, and it does not change Apple's current handling of Advanced Data Protection in the UK. The next hearing will determine how much of the order's content becomes public.
