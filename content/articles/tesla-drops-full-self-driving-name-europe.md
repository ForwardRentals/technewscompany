Tesla has replaced the "Full Self-Driving" name with "Tesla Assisted Driving" on its European websites, according to a Reuters report published October 9, after regulators raised concerns that the original name could suggest cars can drive without human supervision.

## What changed

- The new name is visible on Tesla's sites in Germany, Spain, the Netherlands and Slovakia, and pages that promoted FSD now redirect to Tesla Assisted Driving information
- Germany said Tesla had offered to adopt the new branding
- In the United States, Tesla still sells the software as "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)"
- The software itself has reportedly not changed, and drivers remain legally responsible and must supervise it at all times

## Regulatory backdrop

Sweden's consumer protection authorities asked the national transport regulator to examine whether Tesla's advertising could falsely imply fully autonomous driving. In internal correspondence obtained by Reuters, Italian officials warned the name could lead users to believe the vehicle has unsupervised driving functions.

Eight EU countries have already authorized the system, and Slovakia is expected to become the ninth within days, per the report. France is conducting further testing.

## Why it matters

An EU vote that could clear wider deployment had been expected in October but has been postponed until at least December. The renaming does not guarantee approval. Tesla has urged European owners to contact national regulators and ask for faster decisions, Reuters reported.

Tesla has positioned driver assistance, robotaxis and software as central to its long-term strategy, and EU clearance would open a market for paid driver-assistance features. Tesla's system requires constant supervision and falls short of SAE Level 3, which allows hands-off operation under defined conditions.
