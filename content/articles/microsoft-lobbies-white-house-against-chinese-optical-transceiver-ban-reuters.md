Microsoft is reportedly lobbying the White House against a possible US ban on imports of Chinese-made optical transceivers, according to a Reuters report dated November 10, 2026 and relayed by Silicon UK. Reuters cited unnamed people familiar with the matter.

## The details

Optical transceivers are the components that connect servers and networking gear at high speed inside data centres. According to the report, Microsoft was among several companies that met officials from the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy and the National Security Council in September.

- A Morgan Stanley research note in October said the administration was weighing a ban on high-speed transceivers above a set speed threshold.
- The technology in question is not expected to reach volume production until 2028, which the article says would give US makers time to ramp up.
- In August, the Information Technology Industry Council wrote to the FCC asking it not to add optical transceivers to a trade blacklist, citing the effect of supply disruptions on data-centre deployment.

## Why it matters

The lobbying argues that a ban could weaken the US in the AI race against China, per the report. Counterpoint Research estimates Chinese companies control about two-thirds of the global optical transceiver market, so any restriction would reshape supply chains for cloud operators.

The issue sits alongside other optical-sector moves this month, including Marvell's announced plan to acquire Lumentum. No ban has been announced, and Microsoft has not publicly commented in the coverage reviewed.
