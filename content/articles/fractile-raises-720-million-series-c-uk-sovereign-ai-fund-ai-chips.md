Fractile, a London-based AI chip start-up, has raised $720 million (about £542 million) in a Series C round that includes the UK's Sovereign AI Fund, according to Silicon UK, which reported the deal on November 12.

## The round

The UK government put Fractile's valuation at more than £5 billion, roughly $6.6 billion. The Financial Times and Reuters reported a figure of $7 billion, per Silicon UK.

- **Investors:** Besides the Sovereign AI Fund, backers include Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed Venture Partners and Redpoint.
- **Prior round:** A Series B in May reportedly raised $220 million at a valuation of about $1 billion, meaning the new valuation is more than six times higher.
- **Leadership:** Walter Goodwin is the founder and chief executive.
- **Government rationale:** The UK government said the investment is meant to give Britain a larger stake in future AI hardware.

## What Fractile builds

Fractile designs chips for AI inference, the stage where trained models generate answers. According to Silicon UK, its approach targets memory bottlenecks by integrating DRAM into the processor itself. The company plans to launch its first production chip next year.

## Context

Fractile joins a group of richly funded custom-chip start-ups that includes Axelera, Etched and OLIX, Silicon UK noted. Nvidia, the dominant supplier of AI accelerators, bought Groq last year and introduced its own inference chip in March, according to the same report.

Silicon UK did not detail the size of each investor's stake or give revenue figures for Fractile, and the company has not shipped its production silicon yet.
