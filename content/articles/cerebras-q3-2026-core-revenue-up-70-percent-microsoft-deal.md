Cerebras Systems reported third-quarter results on Thursday, Nov. 12, that topped Wall Street estimates and announced a new relationship with Microsoft, CNBC reported. Shares rose about 7% on Friday.

## The numbers

Figures below are as reported by CNBC against LSEG consensus.

- Core revenue: $230.9 million, versus $214.7 million expected, up 70% from $135.7 million a year earlier.
- Adjusted earnings: 12 cents per share, versus an expected loss of 14 cents.
- Growth slowed from the 103% core revenue increase in the second quarter.
- Fourth-quarter core revenue guidance: $310 million to $330 million, versus about $271 million expected.
- Fourth-quarter core gross margin guidance: 47% to 49%, versus 42.1% expected.

Cerebras also said it expects revenue to triple in 2027, implying about $2.85 billion. That is slightly below the $2.92 billion analysts had projected, per LSEG.

## Microsoft and other customers

Cerebras disclosed the Microsoft deal on its analyst call. Microsoft signed in October, and CEO Andrew Feldman said, "We believe revenue begins to show up in Q1 '27." Cerebras has also reached agreements with Amazon and Oracle, CNBC noted.

Feldman said long lead times and higher costs for GPUs create an opening for Cerebras hardware sales. He added that the company is evaluating financing options to meet rising demand.

## Context

Cerebras listed on Nasdaq in May 2026 under the ticker CBRS. CNBC reports the stock is down roughly 47% since its debut despite Friday's gain. The report came the same day Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned, which dominated market attention and has prompted a rotation across chip stocks.

## What to watch

Investors will look for the first Microsoft-related revenue in the first quarter of 2027 and for details of any financing Cerebras pursues to expand capacity.
