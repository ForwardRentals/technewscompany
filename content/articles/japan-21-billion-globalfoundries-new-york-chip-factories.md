Japan plans to invest up to $21 billion in two semiconductor factories in New York that GlobalFoundries will operate, according to Reuters, with the U.S. government and Japanese interests set to jointly own the plants.

## Deal details

- **Capital:** Japan would provide all of the investment, GlobalFoundries said, while the U.S. takes a stake in the new factories.
- **Timeline:** Construction of the first factory is scheduled to begin this year, with chip production expected in 2029. The partners will decide whether to build the second before the end of 2028.
- **Scale:** The first factory is expected to add capacity for about 900,000 300mm wafers a year and create more than 1,000 factory jobs.
- **U.S. stake:** The U.S. government will take a 4% stake in GlobalFoundries, lifting its total holding to about 7%. Abu Dhabi's Mubadala Investment remains the majority owner.

## Customers

GlobalFoundries CEO Tim Breen said the company would manage customer relationships and that initial customers include Japan's Renesas Electronics and Apple. He said the company would like to give priority to U.S. and Japanese customers.

## Context

The project falls under the arrangement struck last year in which Japan committed to invest $550 billion in the United States in return for lower U.S. tariffs on Japanese cars and other goods, from at least 25% to 15%. Under that arrangement, President Trump selects the projects Japan funds.

The "up to" wording matters: the second factory is not yet committed. The announcement follows a five-year interposer agreement between GlobalFoundries and TSMC disclosed last week.
