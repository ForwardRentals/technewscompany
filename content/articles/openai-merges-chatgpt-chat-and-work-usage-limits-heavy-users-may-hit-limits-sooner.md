OpenAI has begun rolling out a unified Chat and Work experience in ChatGPT in which both modes draw on a single shared usage allowance, TechCrunch reported on Wednesday. The company acknowledged that a small share of its heaviest users may reach their limits sooner than before.

## How the change works

The rollout started with a small group of Pro subscribers, according to a post on X by OpenAI's Thibault "Tibo" Sottiaux, and followed an earlier email to users. OpenAI's support page says shared limits will reach a wider audience in December.

- Chat and Work will share one usage meter across ChatGPT.
- Conversations using the Instant model remain unlimited and do not count toward the shared allowance.
- Users can keep using Instant after they exhaust their other limits.

TechCrunch said an OpenAI spokesperson expects most users not to see more frequent usage resets, while "a small fraction of its heaviest users may hit their limits sooner."

## Subscriber concerns

Some subscribers have voiced worry that combining the meters could reduce their access to advanced reasoning models while subscription prices stay the same. OpenAI said it will keep working on efficiency so people can get more from their plans.

The report did not specify exact usage caps, any pricing changes, or which plans beyond Pro will receive the update and when.

## Context

TechCrunch noted that Anthropic made a similar move in September, combining its Claude chat and Cowork tools into a single interface. The publication linked such changes to rising infrastructure costs and growing demand for more capable models, which put pressure on how AI providers allocate compute among subscribers.

For users, the practical effect depends on how people split their time between conversational and agent-style work. Someone who relied on separate allowances for each mode may find the combined meter lasts less time, while lighter users may notice no difference. OpenAI has not published figures that would let subscribers estimate the impact in advance.
