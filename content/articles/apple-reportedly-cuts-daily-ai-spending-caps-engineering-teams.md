Apple is reducing how much its employees can spend on AI tools each day, according to a Bloomberg report relayed by TechCrunch on November 4. TechCrunch said it contacted Apple for comment; no response was included in its article.

## What Bloomberg reported

Citing its sources, Bloomberg said:

- In October, the daily AI spending cap for engineering teams was lowered from $300 to $200.
- More reductions are planned, with some allowances falling to $100 a day.
- For staff outside engineering, Apple is considering a daily limit as low as $25.

Apple has not publicly confirmed the changes.

## Why it matters

Companies spent much of 2026 encouraging workers to use AI heavily, a trend TechCrunch called "tokenmaxxing." The outlet wrote that enthusiasm cooled as bills arrived, and that some firms have started to consider rationing usage as a more cost-conscious approach. The report also pointed to continued concern that the cost of building and running AI outpaces its often hard-to-measure productivity gains.

Apple has explored generative AI in its engineering work, including for chip design, according to earlier reporting linked in the TechCrunch piece.

## Context

The reported cuts come as Apple manages higher component costs. In its latest earnings, [inventory rose 154 percent as memory prices climbed](/news/apple-inventory-up-154-percent-memory-costs-ternus-earnings/). Apple also continues to roll out AI features to customers, including [Siri language expansion in iOS 27.2](/news/apple-ios-27-2-siri-ai-languages-160-security-fixes/).

Because the details come from unnamed sources, the figures may differ from Apple's actual internal policy.
