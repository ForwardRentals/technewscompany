Nebius said with its third-quarter results on Thursday that it plans about $40 billion in spending in 2027, above what Wall Street expected, while trimming its 2026 revenue forecast because of delays at data centers it does not control, TechCrunch reported. Shares fell 17% on the day.

## Results and guidance

- **Q3 revenue:** $981 million, up 571% year over year; about a third came from a one-time payment tied to a new partnership model.
- **2026 revenue forecast:** cut by $200 million to $3 billion.
- **2027 revenue forecast:** $12 billion to $13 billion.
- **Cash:** $7.4 billion at quarter end.
- **Profitability:** pre-tax break-even expected by the fourth quarter.

## How it will pay for the buildout

Nebius said customer prepayments and loans backed by customer contracts should cover up to 85% of 2027 capital spending. Its CFO named Microsoft and Meta contracts as eligible for that financing. CEO Arkady Volozh said: "Building at this scale is hard, and delays are part of our industry right now."

## The Microsoft contract and the New Jersey delay

Microsoft is among Nebius's largest customers, with a contract worth up to $19.4 billion. Capacity at a Vineland, New Jersey site run by third-party operator DataOne is months late; just over half of Microsoft's capacity is now planned by the end of 2026 and the rest in the second quarter of 2027. Nebius attributed the delay to the site winding down temporary power generation faster than expected while switching to Bloom Energy fuel cells.

TechCrunch reported that Floodlight found at least 45 unpermitted natural gas generators there in August, and state regulators fined the facility $1.1 million. Under Nebius's summary of the contract, Microsoft can terminate affected service after a grace period if dates are missed and no alternative is offered. Nebius said Microsoft has been part of the planning; Microsoft has not publicly canceled anything.

Nebius says its own data centers are on schedule or ahead and will supply most new capacity from 2027. Related: [Microsoft reportedly using fewer neoclouds](/news/microsoft-reportedly-to-use-fewer-neoclouds-ai-buildout/).
