Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co.'s board of directors approved plans on November 10 to invest up to $500 million each in Anthropic and OpenAI, a combined ceiling of $1 billion, according to a report summarizing the company's filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

## What the board approved

- **Anthropic:** up to $500 million in shares bought at the IPO price through a private placement that runs alongside the offering.
- **OpenAI:** up to $500 million in preferred stock. TSMC did not disclose a price or timing for that purchase.
- The approvals are maximum amounts, not confirmation that either transaction has closed.

The Anthropic placement closes right after the IPO and only if the offering goes ahead. StockAnalysis.com calculated that at the $74 midpoint of Anthropic's proposed $70 to $78 range, the stake would equal roughly 6.8 million shares, or about 0.05% of the company. That figure is the outlet's own estimate.

## Part of a larger placement

TSMC Partners is one of seven technology companies agreeing to buy an aggregate $26 billion of non-voting Class N shares at the IPO price, according to Anthropic's S-1 amendment as described in the report. Alphabet and Nvidia are the largest buyers at $10 billion each. OpenAI, meanwhile, raised $11 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation on November 6, per Reuters as cited by the outlet.

## Why it matters

TSMC has historically concentrated on contract chip manufacturing rather than taking stakes in its customers' customers. Anthropic's prospectus notes that most of the world's most advanced chipmaking capacity sits in Taiwan, mainly at TSMC, which underlines how dependent AI developers are on the foundry.

The filing as reported does not give a reason for the investments or describe any commercial agreement tied to them. The report also says the board earmarked a separate $500 million for an investment company focused on the semiconductor industry and its supporting ecosystem.

Anthropic's final IPO price and listing date had not been set at the time of the report.
