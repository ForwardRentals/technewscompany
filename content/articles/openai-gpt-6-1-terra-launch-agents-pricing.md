OpenAI on Monday, October 26, released GPT-6.1 Terra, a smaller and lower-cost model aimed at computer use, visual reasoning, coding and everyday work, BleepingComputer reported. OpenAI describes it as a fast, efficient model with detailed visual reasoning and precise computer use.

## Pricing and availability

API pricing is $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens. OpenAI says that is 50% lower than GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol. The model is available through the API as `gpt-6.1-terra`, and to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex.

## What OpenAI claims

All of the following figures come from OpenAI and were not independently verified in the report:

- On the Agents' Last Exam at medium reasoning effort, Terra scored 15 percentage points higher than GPT-5.6 Terra at about one-third of the cost.
- OpenAI says Terra performs on par with GPT-6 Astra at one-tenth of the price per token, and finishes tasks more than three times faster than both GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol.
- On an internal benchmark, the error rate at low reasoning effort fell from 53.6% for GPT-5.6 Terra to 16.7%. At maximum effort it fell by 18 percentage points to 4.3%.
- On Terminal-Bench 4.0 at maximum effort, Terra scored 50.3%, against 21.5% for GPT-5.6 Terra.

OpenAI still recommends GPT-6.1 Sol or GPT-6 Astra for the most complex coding tasks.

## Target uses

The company points to tasks such as filling out forms, editing video, interpreting technical drawings and automating repetitive desktop work.

## Context

The release follows OpenAI's earlier GPT-6 launch covered on this site. Lower-priced models matter to developers building agents, which can consume large numbers of tokens per task. Benchmark results published by a vendor can differ from results in independent testing.
