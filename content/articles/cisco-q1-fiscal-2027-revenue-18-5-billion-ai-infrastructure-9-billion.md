Cisco reported fiscal first-quarter 2027 revenue of $18.5 billion on Thursday, Nov. 12, beating analyst expectations and raising its forecast for AI infrastructure sales to hyperscale cloud customers, CNBC reported.

## The results

Comparisons to estimates use LSEG consensus as cited by CNBC.

- Revenue: $18.5 billion, versus $18.1 billion expected and up 24% from $14.88 billion a year earlier.
- Adjusted earnings: $1.40 per share, versus $1.33 expected.
- GAAP net income: $4.6 billion, or $1.14 per share, up from $2.9 billion, or 72 cents per share, a year earlier.

## AI infrastructure outlook

Cisco raised its fiscal-year forecast for AI infrastructure revenue from hyperscalers to $9 billion, which CNBC says is more than double the level of fiscal 2026. Hyperscalers are the large cloud operators building out AI data centers, and networking equipment such as switches and optics is a core part of those builds.

## Guidance

For the fiscal second quarter, Cisco expects:

- Revenue of $18.0 billion to $18.2 billion, versus a $17.88 billion average analyst estimate.
- Adjusted earnings of $1.34 to $1.36 per share, versus a $1.23 average estimate.

CEO Chuck Robbins said, "Organizations are turning to Cisco for our trusted, market-defining technology."

## Market reaction

Shares rose in extended trading after the report, though CNBC did not give a figure. The stock was up about 35% for the year as of Thursday's close, compared with roughly 9% for the Nasdaq over the same period.

## Context

The report landed on a day dominated by Nvidia's leadership change. Data center networking parts are also at the center of a separate policy debate over a possible U.S. restriction on Chinese optical transceivers.
