CrowdStrike and Zscaler announced on November 23 that CrowdStrike will acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valued at about $43.9 billion, according to TechCrunch's report on the announcement.

## Deal terms

The deal has not closed. The companies expect completion around November 2027, subject to regulatory approval. TechCrunch's article did not state per-share terms.

## What the companies say

CrowdStrike sells endpoint protection, which secures devices such as laptops and phones. Zscaler, based in San Jose, California, sells cloud security products built on zero-trust principles, which require users to re-authenticate each time they access an internal system. Zscaler serves about 8,000 enterprise customers.

According to the report, the companies said the combination would:

- Create the "largest single addressable market" in cybersecurity.
- Offer customers "superior protection."
- Help organizations adopt AI safely.

## Context

Security vendors have been consolidating products onto single platforms as customers try to reduce the number of tools they manage. The companies expect the transaction to take about a year to close, pending regulatory approval.

The announcement came the same day that Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, a week in which technology stocks were volatile.
