Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang apologized to employees on Tuesday, November 24, at the company's first all-hands meeting since the board reinstated him a day earlier, according to reports from CNBC, The Wall Street Journal, Wired and TechCrunch. The accounts come from people in the room; Nvidia did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment, and no full transcript has been published.

## What Huang reportedly said

- He told staff, "I hurt all of you," CNBC reported, and said he would do better "the second time around."
- The Wall Street Journal, as relayed by TechCrunch, quoted him as saying it was "the hardest company meeting I've ever done," and that the hardest meeting of his life was with his wife.
- He acknowledged breaking the company's code of conduct, which he largely wrote. Wired reported that he said the code remains in force but that mitigating circumstances matter, and that a violation does not mean an employee is immediately shown the door.
- He said he is in therapy and is grateful for a second chance, according to TechCrunch.
- He thanked Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress for leading the company as interim CEO.
- CNBC reported that he said Nvidia does not need succession planning in the conventional sense, arguing that capable leaders run different parts of the business and that a formal plan would add unnecessary hierarchy.

## Government role

The Wall Street Journal reported that Huang thanked the administration for quick access to intelligence, which he said helped the board judge the national security risks of bringing him back as low. TechCrunch, citing the New York Times, noted that President Donald Trump called the board directly to argue for the reinstatement, and that Huang played down Trump's role in the meeting.

## Background and what comes next

Huang resigned on November 12 amid reports about his personal relationships, including one with a businesswoman alleged to have ties to Chinese intelligence, CNBC reported. The board reinstated him on November 23. CNBC said Nvidia's shares rose more than 30% on the day he returned but fell on Tuesday.

Scrutiny is continuing. TechCrunch reported that Huang is due to appear at a public Senate Banking Committee hearing and, in the coming weeks, at closed House hearings before the Intelligence, Foreign Affairs and China Select committees.
