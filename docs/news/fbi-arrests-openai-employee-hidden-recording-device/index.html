FBI Arrests OpenAI Employee Accused of Hiding a Recording Device in a Conference Room

Prosecutors say the product-team employee placed an audio device behind a TV in an executive meeting room; OpenAI says it sees no sign of stolen model data.

By TNC NewsdeskOctober 13, 2026 · 2 min read
OpenAISecurity

The FBI has arrested an OpenAI employee accused of planting a hidden recording device in an executive conference room at the company's San Francisco office, according to TechCrunch, which reported the case on October 13, 2026.

The allegations

Prosecutors with the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of California charged Abdulrahman Alfozan, a Saudi national on OpenAI's product team, with using an electronic device to intercept oral communications. The charge carries a maximum sentence of five years in prison. OpenAI spokesperson Nate Evans confirmed that Alfozan joined the company in July 2025.

  • Prosecutors say the device was inserted into a USB port on the back of a television in the conference room.
  • OpenAI had placed a camera that allegedly caught Alfozan looking behind the TV.
  • OpenAI discovered the device in early October, and Alfozan was fired and arrested on October 9, according to TechCrunch.
  • Neither Alfozan nor his attorney could be reached for comment.

OpenAI's response

OpenAI alerted the FBI and used its own AI models to analyze the device, which it said had "advanced encryption, audio monitoring capabilities, and a mechanism for transferring data designed to evade monitoring." In a post on X, the company said it had "no indications" that Alfozan accessed or stole model weights, source code or customer data.

Motive unclear

The purpose of the device has not been established. FBI Director Kash Patel said the scheme aimed to "capture sensitive communications about the company's super intelligence technology," and described protecting American AI innovation as a top counterintelligence priority. TechCrunch noted it is unclear how technically distinct the device is from other commercially available recorders.

The case is at an early stage. A charge is an allegation, and Alfozan is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in court.
