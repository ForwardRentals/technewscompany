Bessent Calls for "Dramatic Shakeup" at Nvidia and an Outside CEO

The Treasury Secretary questioned export-control compliance at the chipmaker days after Jensen Huang resigned, CNBC reports.

By TNC NewsdeskNovember 15, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Kevin McCarthy / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called on Sunday for a "dramatic shakeup" at Nvidia and said its board should look outside the company for its next chief executive, according to CNBC.

What Bessent said

Speaking on Fox News Sunday, Bessent said other Nvidia executives should also face scrutiny over export-control compliance. He asked whether the company had been pressing "seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings?" He also questioned how Nvidia reports revenue from Singapore as its China sales fell. CNBC notes Nvidia stopped reporting Singapore revenue separately in November 2025, saying a customer-headquarters method better reflects its sales geography.

CNBC's article contains no direct Nvidia response to the remarks.

Background

  • November 12: Jensen Huang resigned and Nvidia named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO.
  • The Megaspeed link: Huang's departure followed revelations about his relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China. CNBC reports U.S. intelligence officials suspect her of spying for China. Megaspeed said she left in 2025 and has launched an internal probe.
  • Washington friction: Bessent told CNBC on Saturday that several agencies had supported adding Megaspeed to the export blacklist for months, adding, "But Commerce has the pen." Axios reported a confrontation between Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick; Commerce denied it, and a White House official said President Trump has "complete confidence" in Lutnick.

What comes next

Nvidia is scheduled to report its first quarterly results since Huang's exit on or around November 17. CNBC also reported that Cantor Fitzgerald, formerly led by Lutnick, has helped arrange financing for Nvidia's partners; Cantor said the related Nvidia share positions stem from client swap transactions, not proprietary investments, and CNBC said there is no evidence Lutnick shared sensitive information with his sons, who now lead the firm.
