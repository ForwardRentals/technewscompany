Nvidia Board Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO Less Than Two Weeks After His Resignation

The company said the decision followed input from the White House; Semafor reports the vote was unanimous and that President Trump dialed into the board meeting.

By TNC NewsdeskNovember 23, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Maurizio Pesce / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Nvidia announced on Monday, November 23, that its board has reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, about two weeks after he resigned amid scrutiny over his personal conduct and ties to a company at the center of a chip-diversion investigation.

How the decision came together

According to Semafor, which cited a person familiar with the matter, the board met late Sunday and voted unanimously to bring Huang back. Nvidia said the decision was based on input from the White House. Board chairman Harvey Jones said in a statement quoted by Semafor: "The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA."

Semafor reported that President Donald Trump dialed into the meeting to argue for the reinstatement, and White House communications director Steven Cheung told the outlet that Trump was "totally involved." Trump told CNBC in a phone interview that he "made the deal." CNBC's sources said senior administration officials had assured the board that Huang had not broken U.S. national security laws. That assurance has not been independently confirmed, and several Republicans questioned it, Semafor reported.

Background

  • Huang resigned on November 12, and Colette Kress, the company's chief financial officer, became interim CEO.
  • On November 17, Jones said the board did not intend to reinstate him, CNBC reported.
  • TechCrunch reported that employees applauded Huang on his return to headquarters, and that he told them: "Thank you all for the second chance."
  • Semafor reported that Nvidia's senior leadership learned of the decision only shortly before it became public.

What remains open

Congressional scrutiny is continuing. Semafor reported that Huang is expected to appear privately before House members and publicly before senators next week, and that Senator Elizabeth Warren called the reinstatement "reckless." The Justice Department is also investigating Nvidia customers over possible export-control violations, according to Semafor.

Semafor reported that Nvidia's shares jumped about 30% on Monday, their largest one-day gain in more than two decades. That figure comes from a single outlet and has not been checked against exchange data.

Nvidia's board had earlier commissioned an independent review related to Huang and Megaspeed. This article will be updated as the company or Congress releases further details.
