Anthropic Report Says Its Workforce Has Become Less Junior and Spends More Time in Meetings

The company's own data shows junior technical staff falling from 21% to 14% of employees, with meeting time per person up 20% year over year.

By TNC NewsdeskOctober 17, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: TechCrunch / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Anthropic published a report on October 17 describing how AI has changed its own workforce, finding that the company has fewer junior technical employees and more meetings, TechCrunch reported.

Hiring and headcount

According to the report as summarized by TechCrunch:

  • Junior-level employees in technical departments fell from 21% to 14% of staff since early 2025.
  • Junior technical hires dropped from 27% to 9% of all hires.
  • Technical teams shrank from 61% to 56% of the workforce.

Anthropic attributed the last change to the natural expansion of business functions as the company scales, and to coding tools that let non-technical staff build software.

Productivity and attention

In an April 2026 survey, 27% of employees said Claude made them more productive, and 20% said their jobs had shifted from doing work to managing Claude instances. One unnamed engineer likened it to babysitting. The report calls human attention and judgment a bottleneck, because Claude produces a large volume of text for people to read and organize.

Anthropic pointed to more code per person, more sales per representative and more contracts processed as signs of higher output. It acknowledged that more code does not necessarily mean better code, but said it saw no sign of declining quality.

Meetings

Calendar data showed meeting time per employee up 20% year over year, and up 16% for technical contributors. Anthropic linked this to human trust and accountability, with employees describing face-to-face coordination as indispensable.

Caveats

TechCrunch noted that the report's survey sample size, question wording and response rate are not described in the article, and its employee comments are anonymous. The figures come from Anthropic's own analysis of its own staff rather than an independent study.
