Nvidia Reportedly Tells Partners to Pause Server Shipments to Four Southeast Asian Countries

According to the Wall Street Journal, Dell and Super Micro were among the manufacturers asked to hold shipments while Nvidia checks that regional partners meet U.S. export rules.

By TNC NewsdeskNovember 26, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Coolcaesar / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Nvidia has reportedly told its manufacturing partners to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, according to the Wall Street Journal as relayed by TechCrunch on November 26.

What was reported

The instruction reportedly went to server makers including Dell and Super Micro. The reports say Nvidia is assessing whether its regional partners comply with U.S. export requirements. No length for the pause was given, and TechCrunch's write-up did not include an on-record statement from Nvidia.

Why the region matters

The move follows a rule change earlier this month. The U.S. Commerce Department closed a loophole that let Chinese companies reach Nvidia chips remotely through data centers in third countries, according to reporting by NBC News and The Washington Post cited by TechCrunch. The Post reportedly described the closure as grudging.

Key points from the reporting:

  • The affected countries are Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia.
  • The pause covers server shipments routed through manufacturing partners.
  • The Institute for Progress, a think tank, estimates that remote data centers of this kind supply about 24% of China's AI computing power.

Context

Southeast Asia has become a major location for AI data center construction, partly because operators there can host advanced chips for customers elsewhere. The new Commerce rule, covered earlier on this site, changed the licensing picture for such remote access.

The pause comes during a turbulent week for Nvidia, which reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive on Monday. The shipment report has not been confirmed by Nvidia, and details such as which orders are affected remain unclear.
