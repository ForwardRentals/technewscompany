Nvidia Board Reportedly Meets on Whether to Remove CEO Jensen Huang, Shares Fall After Hours

CNBC confirmed the board was meeting late Wednesday after reports of two relationships; Nvidia shares fell roughly 8% to 9% in extended trading.

By TNC NewsdeskNovember 12, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Anderseidesvik / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Nvidia's board was meeting late on Wednesday, November 11, to decide whether to remove co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang, CNBC confirmed, after the Wall Street Journal reported the board was preparing to force him out. Nvidia shares fell more than 9% in after-hours trading, according to CNBC; a Reuters analysis put the drop at about 8%. Nvidia had not commented publicly on the reports at the time of writing.

What has been reported

The board has opened an independent investigation and hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to examine allegations that Huang was involved with a woman U.S. intelligence officials believe works for China's Ministry of State Security, according to reporting by the Financial Times and the New York Times cited by CNBC.

The New York Times also reported that Huang had a romantic relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare. Nvidia and Powell did not respond to CNBC's requests for comment.

In an email to staff on Sunday, Huang wrote: "I was unfaithful to my wife." CNBC reported that he made the admission days before the Powell report appeared.

Market reaction

Reuters reported that Nvidia customers fell in after-hours trading, with CoreWeave down more than 6% and Korea's Naver down nearly 6%, while Broadcom and AMD rose. Chinese GPU makers Moore Threads, MetaX and Cambricon each rose nearly 10% on Thursday.

  • Nvidia is a roughly $4 trillion chipmaker, per Reuters.
  • Its latest earnings report listed about $164.5 billion in financial commitments, including an obligation tied to an OpenAI data center in Ohio capped at $105 billion.
  • CNBC said the stock was on pace for a sixth straight weekly decline before the after-hours drop.

Why it matters

Huang has not named a successor. Analysts quoted by Reuters pointed to Ian Buck and CFO Colette Kress as possible internal candidates, and said Nvidia's products and near-term revenue are unlikely to change quickly. "The AI trade rests on that demand, not on one individual," Anna Wu of Floodlines Research told Reuters.

CNBC reported that about 800 million Nvidia shares are held in trusts co-owned with Huang's wife, Lori, while he personally holds roughly 70 million shares.

Nvidia is due to report earnings next week, when leadership and strategy are expected to be a focus. The board's decision had not been announced at the time of writing, and the underlying reports rest on anonymous sourcing from the outlets named above.
