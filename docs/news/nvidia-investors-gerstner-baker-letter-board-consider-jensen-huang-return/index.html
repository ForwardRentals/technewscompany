Two Major Nvidia Investors Urge Board to Weigh Possible Return of Jensen Huang

Altimeter's Brad Gerstner and Atreides's Gavin Baker asked the board for a "slow, measured, and deliberate process" on leadership after Huang's resignation.

By TNC NewsdeskNovember 13, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Will Buckner / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Two prominent Nvidia shareholders have publicly asked the company's board to keep all options open on its next leader, including a possible return of co-founder Jensen Huang, CNBC reported. The letter, signed by Altimeter Capital CEO Brad Gerstner and Atreides Management's Gavin Baker, was posted on X on Friday, Nov. 13, a day after Huang resigned as president, CEO and director.

What the letter says

The investors called for a "slow, measured, and deliberate process regarding the future leadership of Nvidia," according to CNBC. They wrote that interim CEO Colette Kress is capable of running the company in the meantime and argued that Huang deserves a considered process rather than a rushed replacement. The letter stops short of demanding his immediate reinstatement.

Nvidia had not responded to the letter in the CNBC article.

The investors' stakes

As of June 30, the most recent figures cited by CNBC:

  • Altimeter held 9.4 million Nvidia shares, worth nearly $1.9 billion, its largest disclosed equity position.
  • Atreides held 1.2 million shares, worth nearly $250 million.
  • Together, the two positions were valued at about $2.13 billion.

Background

Huang resigned on Nov. 12 amid an independent board investigation and press reports about his personal relationships, including one with Alice Huang, who has been described in reports as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. Those claims are allegations in press reports and have not been independently confirmed. Nvidia's announcement did not give a reason. The board named Kress, its chief financial officer for 13 years, as interim CEO.

CNBC also reported that Elon Musk said he had signed a "Stand with Jensen" petition, which claims more than 100,000 signatures, including over 5,000 current Nvidia employees. Those signature figures are the petition's own claims. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is scheduled to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 10, per the same report.

Why it matters

Nvidia is among the world's most valuable companies, and its board has said it has not yet chosen a search firm for a permanent CEO. Public pressure from sizable holders adds a new variable to a succession process that the company has not put on a timeline.
