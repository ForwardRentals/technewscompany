Google to Turn Off Search for Student Accounts at K-12 Schools That Ban AI, Report Says

From early 2027, Google's AI products will be off by default for K-12 student accounts until administrators enable them.

By TNC NewsdeskNovember 3, 2026 · 2 min read
GoogleAI

Google said on November 3 that it will switch off its AI tools by default for K-12 student accounts starting in early 2027, and that students at schools that reject AI entirely will lose access to Google Search on school-managed accounts, according to Tech Startups. The outlet cites a Google blog post titled "AI learning era updates 2026" and comments by a Google executive to The New York Times.

What is changing

  • Beginning in early 2027, Gemini, NotebookLM, Gemini in Classroom and AI features in Search will be off for K-12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly turn them on.
  • Schools that opt out of AI altogether will see Search become unavailable to students on Google-managed accounts, who will get a message saying so.
  • The restriction applies to school-managed K-12 accounts, not personal accounts or other search engines.
  • Google plans to share "more specifics" with schools in the coming months before the changes roll out, per the report.

Tech Startups attributes to Chris Phillips, Google's vice president for education, the explanation that "Search and AI are deeply integrated," which is why a school cannot easily keep one and drop the other.

Guided Learning expansion

Google also expanded Guided Learning, its AI tutoring feature, which is now available globally in Google Classroom. The company plans to test it in Search at selected U.S. schools later this month, according to the report.

Context

Tech Startups notes, citing The Wall Street Journal, that school districts cannot turn off AI Overviews and AI Mode in Search through standard administrator controls, and that some districts have built their own workarounds. Earlier this year, the child-safety group Common Sense Media criticized the lack of a way to disable AI in Search for student accounts without blocking Search itself; Google disputed aspects of that review.

Google's Chromebooks and tools such as Classroom, Docs, Gmail and Search are widely used in U.S. public schools, so the policy could affect districts that have chosen to restrict generative AI.

Details here come from Tech Startups' summary; Google's original post was not independently accessible when this was written.
