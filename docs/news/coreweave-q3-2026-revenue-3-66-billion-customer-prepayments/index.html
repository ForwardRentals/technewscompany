CoreWeave Q3 Revenue Rises 168% to $3.66 Billion as It Leans on Customer Prepayments

The AI cloud provider reported a $709 million net loss and a $132.5 billion backlog, and said it will not fund growth in the unsecured debt market at current yields.

By TNC NewsdeskNovember 11, 2026 · 2 min read
CoreWeaveCloud & Infrastructure

CoreWeave reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $3.66 billion, up 168% from a year earlier, and said it expects about $9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months, TechCrunch reported. The company posted a net loss of $709 million.

The numbers

  • Revenue: $3.66 billion, up 168% year over year
  • Net loss: $709 million
  • Quarterly interest expense: $846 million
  • Backlog: $132.5 billion
  • Expected prepayments and deposits: about $9 billion over three months, including more than $3 billion tied to new contracts for capacity it has already built

A shift in financing

CoreWeave is moving away from unsecured debt toward loans backed by specific customer contracts. CEO Michael Intrator said: "We will not use the unsecured debt market to fund our business at current yields, nor do we need to." TechCrunch noted that yields on some CoreWeave bonds rose above 15% last month, reflecting investor concern about debt-funded data center buildouts.

In August the company closed a $2.6 billion loan called DDTL 5.5, its first designed for shorter-term customer contracts, which lets it borrow in installments. TechCrunch said peers Nebius and IREN have also relied on large prepayments from Microsoft.

Pricing

Intrator said capacity prices rose about 25% in July and another 10% later in the quarter, and are still climbing, per TechCrunch.

  • Short-term capacity is selling above $40 million per megawatt a year.
  • New infrastructure on four- and five-year contracts is priced at $20 million to $25 million per megawatt.

Trade-offs

Shorter contracts can command higher prices, but TechCrunch noted that lenders generally prefer long-term contracts because they give more predictable cash flow, and that upfront payments could lock in prices that look less attractive if demand keeps growing.

CoreWeave shares fell more than 6% in after-hours trading on Wednesday amid the Nvidia leadership reports, according to Reuters.
