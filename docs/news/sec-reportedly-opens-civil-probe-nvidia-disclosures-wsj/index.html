SEC Reportedly Opens Civil Probe Into Nvidia's Investor Disclosures, WSJ Says

The agency has not confirmed the inquiry, which was reported days after Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive; Nvidia had not commented in coverage reviewed.

By TNC NewsdeskNovember 17, 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Will Buckner / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons

The Securities and Exchange Commission has opened a civil investigation into Nvidia, the Wall Street Journal reported on November 17, citing people familiar with the matter. According to the report, as relayed by PYMNTS, the inquiry concerns whether the company's disclosures misled investors.

What is known

The reports do not specify which disclosures are under review, and none reviewed said whether the probe is linked to Chief Executive Jensen Huang's resignation the previous week. An SEC spokesperson told PYMNTS the agency "does not comment on the existence or nonexistence of a possible investigation." Nvidia had not replied to PYMNTS at the time of publication. A civil probe is a fact-finding step and does not itself mean a violation was found or charges will follow.

Surrounding events

The report landed amid a turbulent week for the chipmaker:

  • Huang resigned on November 12, and Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, became interim CEO.
  • The Justice and Commerce departments issued a joint statement confirming an investigation into Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company that PYMNTS said is suspected of diverting Nvidia products to China.
  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said he wanted answers from the board and outside counsel within 10 days.
  • Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion on November 17, in its first results under Kress.

Huang has apologized publicly. PYMNTS reported that multiple outlets said the board had no interest in reinstating him.

Context

Nvidia previously agreed in 2022 to pay a $5.5 million penalty to settle SEC charges over disclosures about crypto-mining demand, without admitting or denying the findings. The current matter is unconfirmed and rests on unnamed sources.
